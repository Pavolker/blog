Ourselves

In culture, the reaction is more intense because we still imagine art as the last inviolable territory. When a machine reorganizes language, images, and sounds convincingly, it does not only threaten jobs. It threatens the fiction that the author creates alone, from within himself, something that did not belong to anyone. I do not believe in that fiction.

Creativity is not the production of the without-antecedent. It is the actualization of possibilities within a heritage that exceeds us. A work is not less human because it was mediated by algorithm. It is human because it only exists within the long human chain that produced its elements, its instruments, its circulation, and its meanings.

There is no alien in the human cultural cauldron. There is humanity producing increasingly powerful tools to reorganize its own heritage. The challenge is not to defend an imaginary purity of human creation against a foreign intelligence. The challenge is to prevent a small portion of humanity from capturing the common stock, controlling the tools of cultural reorganization, and transforming a collective heritage into a private monopoly.

The asymmetry of credit has always existed. The difference is that now we have instruments that force us to see it. And, once we see it, we can no longer pretend that creation is a solitary, originary act, disconnected from the stock that made it possible.

The question is not whether AI will replace artists. The question is whether we will continue to believe the story artists tell about themselves — the story of the solitary genius who pulls works out of nothing — or whether we will finally recognize that creation has always been a collective process, and that AI, in this sense, is not a novelty. It is a mirror.

AI does not place us before something that is not human. It places us before ourselves — before the collective, ancestral, imitative, technical, and accumulative dimension of what we have always called creation. What we see in the mirror may trouble us. But that does not make the mirror an alien. It makes it an instrument that forces us to look at who we really are.
