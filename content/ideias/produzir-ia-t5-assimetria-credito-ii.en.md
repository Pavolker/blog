# The Asymmetry of Credit II — Technology and the Arts

In technology, the same story repeats with other names. Thomas Newcomen, a blacksmith from Dartmouth, built in 1712 the first commercially successful steam engine in the world. His machines pumped water from coal mines for decades throughout Great Britain. He died poor in 1729. James Watt, an instrument maker, had the idea of the separate condenser in 1765 while repairing a Newcomen model. The improvement doubled the efficiency. Watt became rich and famous. The power unit "watt" was named in his honor. Newcomen was forgotten.

Printing followed the same path. Johannes Gutenberg is celebrated in the West as the inventor of the printing press. But the first movable type system was invented by Bi Sheng, a Chinese artisan of the Song Dynasty, between 1039 and 1048, using fired clay to create individual characters. In 1377, in Korea, the Jikji was printed with metal movable type — 78 years before Gutenberg's Bible. UNESCO recognized the Jikji as the oldest extant book printed with movable metal type. Gutenberg did not invent movable type. He created a system that worked with the Latin alphabet, and Europe dominated the historical narrative.

Alfred Russel Wallace, a British naturalist, wrote to Charles Darwin in 1858 from an archipelago in Indonesia, describing a theory of evolution by natural selection that he had developed independently. Darwin rushed the publication of "On the Origin of Species" the following year. Darwin went down in history as the father of the theory of evolution. Wallace became a footnote.

Pablo Picasso studied African masks at the Musée d'Ethnographie du Trocadéro in Paris and painted Les Demoiselles d'Avignon in 1907, a landmark of Cubism. Picasso never credited the anonymous African artists. The masks were treated as "tribal art" — without names, without context, without compensation.

In all these cases, the pattern is the same: the credit went to those who came later, to those who had resources, to those at the center of power. Franklin did not have the contacts that Watson and Crick had. Thornton was a woman and Black in segregated 1950s America. Newcomen was a blacksmith without capital. Bi Sheng was a Chinese artisan without academic status. Wallace was on the other side of the world. The asymmetry of credit is not an exception in the history of culture. It is the rule.
