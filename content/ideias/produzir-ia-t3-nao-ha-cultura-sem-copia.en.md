# There Is No Culture Without Copying

If we subjected all of human production to a complete audit of influences, inspirations, small copies, repetitions, and similarities, we would discover something that might displease us: the human being is an immense plagiarist of himself. He repeats words, melodies, narratives, images, formulas, gestures, and beliefs. Not as an accidental failure, but because that is how culture reproduces itself.

A baby learns by copying. He learns to speak, eat, laugh, react, love, obey, disagree, and imagine through imitation. The whole culture works like this: it transmits forms so that they can be repeated, varied, and transformed. There is no culture without copying.

There is copying without transformation, and there is copying that, when it arises in another context, produces a new configuration. This does not eliminate plagiarism. Plagiarism is an ethical, social, and legal category: improper appropriation, concealment of attribution, fraud, violation of agreements and rights. But plagiarism is not synonymous with ancestry. It is not synonymous with influence. It is not synonymous with learning, repeating, or transforming. If every derivation were plagiarism, culture itself would become impossible.

The confusion between plagiarism and influence is one of the great mistakes in the current debate about AI and culture. When a language model generates a text from billions of prior examples, they say it "plagiarizes." But the same is not said of the writer who spent decades reading and internalizing the authors who shaped him. The writer "was influenced." The machine "plagiarized." The difference is not in the mechanism — both operate upon a stock of antecedents. The difference is in the prestige of whoever performs the operation.

It is precisely here that AI makes visible something that has always been before us. It did not introduce recombination into culture. It merely performs it at a scale, speed, and extent that force us to see the recombinatory nature of creation. And this visibility is uncomfortable because it dismantles the fiction of the creative genius who pulls works out of nothing. AI is not a new way of producing culture. It is a new way of seeing how culture has always been produced.
