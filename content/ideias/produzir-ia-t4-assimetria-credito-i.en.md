# The Asymmetry of Credit I — Science and Music

There is a pattern that repeats throughout the history of culture with impressive regularity. People use other people's discoveries, publish, win all the glory, and those who provided the material for the discovery become great unknowns. The history of culture is a gallery of faces who received credit and an anonymous multitude who supplied the stock. I call this the asymmetry of credit.

I begin with the case that seems clearest to me. Rosalind Franklin, an English chemist and crystallographer, took Photo 51 in 1952 — an X-ray diffraction image that revealed the helical structure of DNA. She did not know it, but her photograph contained the key to one of the greatest mysteries of biology. Maurice Wilkins, her colleague at King's College, showed the photo to James Watson without Franklin's knowledge or permission. Watson and Francis Crick used the image to build the double helix model. They published in Nature in 1953. In 1962, Watson, Crick, and Wilkins received the Nobel Prize in Medicine. Franklin was not there. She had died four years earlier, of ovarian cancer, at 37, without knowing that her photograph had changed the course of biology. The Nobel went to those who used it, not to the one who discovered it.

In music, the pattern is even more brutal. Willie Dixon, a Chicago blues composer, wrote "You Need Love" in 1962, recorded by Muddy Waters. In 1969, Led Zeppelin released "Whole Lotta Love" — same lyrics, different melody, zero credit. Dixon sued in 1985. He received an out-of-court settlement and songwriting credit on subsequent pressings. But he spent his life fighting for royalties, lost a leg to diabetes, and died in 1992 without having received even a fraction of what his songs generated.

Big Mama Thornton recorded "Hound Dog" in 1952. It sold five hundred thousand copies. Elvis Presley recorded the same song in 1956. It sold ten million copies. Thornton told Rolling Stone that she received a single royalty check for five hundred dollars. The credit went to those who came later, to those who had resources, to those at the center of power.
