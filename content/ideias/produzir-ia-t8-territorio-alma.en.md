# The Territory of the Soul

There is an asymmetry that I cannot ignore. We accept, without the same hysteria, that automated systems participate in airplane flight, credit decisions, and financial trading. The IMF estimates that algorithmic trading accounts for about 70% of stock transactions in the United States. These systems are criticized, and should be criticized, for their risks and opacities. But it is rarely said that aviation, credit, or the financial markets have ceased to be human because they began to operate through algorithmic mediation.

The case of the bread machine is even more revealing. In the mid-1980s, Matsushita Electric decided to create the world's first automatic bread-making machine. The challenge was to mechanically replicate the kneading movement that an artisan baker makes with his hands. An engineer at the company, Ikuko Tanaka, spent months learning from a master baker at the Osaka International Hotel, observing his gestures, practicing, internalizing the twisting and stretching motion that he himself could not explain in words. She then translated this tacit knowledge into engineering parameters. In 1987, the Home Bakery reached the market and became a worldwide success.

Functionally, in its logic, it works exactly like an AI model. It receives human inputs — the know-how of a flesh-and-blood baker — and produces bread. The engineer did not invent bread. She took knowledge that already existed, externalized it, and incorporated it into a technical artifact. Yet no one took to the streets to say that the Home Bakery's bread was artificial. No one denounced that the machine was destroying the ancient art of breadmaking. The reaction was silence. And then, enthusiasm. People bought the machine and made bread at home.

Now Anthropic inserts invisible watermarks into texts generated by Claude, in compliance with the European Union's AI Act. AI-made text carries a stamp of origin. That which deviates from the norm is marked. Everything that did not conform to the prevailing normality was marked, named apart, separated from the herd. No one demands that a Home Bakery loaf carry a warning that it was not kneaded by human hands. But AI-made text — that one must confess. Why? Because clothing and bread were never the territory where we sustained the fiction of the solitary creator. Text, though — that territory is. Language — that territory is. The watermark is proof that the resistance is not technical. It is ontological. What is marked is not what the machine does. What is marked is what the machine does when it invades the territory we reserve for the soul.
